# Multi-component Signal - Adaptive New Technique for Redistribution Based on Synchronous Squeezing Transformation [with MATLAB Code]

In this paper, we explore the issue of adaptive reassignment of multi-component signals using synchronous squeeze-and-expand transformations. In fact, the results of reassignment by these so-called "orders" depend significantly on the frequency modulation patterns that constitute the signals. This paper introduces a new technique for locally selecting the optimal order of synchronous squeeze-and-expand transformations to represent multi-component signals and studies its performance in mode reconstruction on both simulated and real signals.
Introduction: Linear time-frequency representation (LTFR) is widely used for analyzing multi-component signals (MCS), as it can estimate the instantaneous frequency (IF) of signal components and achieve signal reconstruction. One of the most commonly used LTFRs is the Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT), whose performance in instantaneous frequency estimation and mode reconstruction depends on the choice of analysis window. To overcome this limitation, literature [3] proposes a synchronous squeeze-and-expand transform (FSST) based reassignment technique, while literature [4], [5] also presents alternative techniques aimed at making STFT more adaptive.
However, FSST is only applicable to MCS with modes composed of less frequency modulated components. Therefore, new methods have been developed to consider this aspect, through what are called second-order synchronous squeeze-and-expand transforms (FSST2) [6], [7], and high-order synchronous squeeze-and-expand transforms (FSSTN) to handle problems of phase rapid oscillations in mode reassignment [8]. The main problem faced by all FSSTs is that they are designed for specific types of modes, and if the mode deviates from this ideal case, the reassignment process becomes rapidly inaccurate. It is important to note that another source of inaccuracy is the sensitivity of the instantaneous frequency estimates used in FSST to noise.
The goal of this paper is to find a method for locally selecting the optimal order of synchronous squeezed transform (SST) on the time-frequency plane to represent patterns in multicomponent signals. The proposed criterion is based on analyzing the energy along the SST ridges at different orders. More precisely, we assume that the SST with the highest coefficient amplitude on the ridges is most suitable for representing correlated patterns. Based on this criterion, we have constructed a new adaptive SST, which has been shown to outperform fixed-order SSTs in the reconstruction of both simulated and real multicomponent signals.
The structure of this paper is as follows: In Section 2, we introduce the symbols used throughout the paper and provide an overview of the basics of SST; then, in Section 3, we introduce the new adaptive SST; finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method through various simulation experiments.

Conclusion: In this paper, we propose a novel technique for locally evaluating the optimal order of synchronous squeezed transform representation of multicomponent signals on the time-frequency plane. Our approach to finding this order is based on analyzing the coefficient amplitude along the SST ridges at different orders, and it has been shown to be more effective in pattern reconstruction than fixed-order SSTs. The main assumption of designing this new adaptive method is that the normalized squeezed transform's amplitude estimate on its ridges can estimate the mode amplitude while maintaining a lower level. Although this seems numerically correct in most cases, further mathematical analysis is needed to support this claim, which will be conducted in the near future.
